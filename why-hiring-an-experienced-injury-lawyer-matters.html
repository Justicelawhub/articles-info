Frequently Asked Questions

1. What should I do if I'm injured while working offshore or on the railroad?

Seek medical attention immediately, report the injury through the proper channels, and document everything, including witness information, accident details, and communication with your employer. These records become critical when building a claim.

2. How is a FELA or offshore injury claim different from regular workers' compensation?

Unlike standard workers' compensation, which pays out regardless of fault, FELA and many offshore injury claims require showing that employer negligence contributed to the accident. This means evidence and legal strategy play a much bigger role in the outcome.

3. Can my family file a claim if a loved one was killed in a workplace accident?

Yes. In cases involving railroad or offshore worker fatalities, surviving family members may have legal options to pursue compensation for their losses, including lost income, loss of companionship, and other damages.

4. How long do I have to file a claim after a workplace injury?

Deadlines vary depending on the type of claim and jurisdiction, so it's important to speak with an attorney as soon as possible after an accident. Waiting too long can result in losing your right to recover compensation.

5. Will hiring an attorney cost me anything upfront?

No. Cases are handled on a contingency fee basis, meaning there are no upfront costs and no fees unless the case is won.
